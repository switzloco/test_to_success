Description
2027 Theme: Combine to NFL Performance
Every spring, college football's top prospects gather in Indianapolis for the NFL Scouting Combine. For decades, player evaluation at the Combine has relied on standardized stopwatch timings and physical testing numbers, such as the 40-yard dash, short shuttle, 3-cone drill, and vertical jump. While these numbers provide a baseline measure of straight-line speed and explosiveness, player tracking sensors worn during workouts now capture the full details of how athletes move throughout each drill.

The 2027 Big Data Bowl provides player tracking data from the NFL Scouting Combine alongside regular-season NFL game tracking data. The goal of this year's competition is to better understand how player movement during Combine drills translates to performance on the field during the NFL regular season.

In this competition, participants are tasked with analyzing prospect movement in a way that is actionable and accessible to coaches, scouts, and front offices. This could include creating a new metric or comparing movement traits across position groups. As is often the case in Big Data Bowl analytics competitions, participants are encouraged to focus on one small aspect of movement, a specific position group, or an individual drill, rather than attempting to evaluate every prospect across all drills.

Examples to consider
Your challenge is generating actionable, practical, and novel insights from Combine tracking data and exploring how drill performance translates to on-field NFL play. Examples include, but are not limited to:

Route running and coverage: Measuring change of direction, deceleration into breaks, and acceleration out of cuts for receivers and defensive backs compared to in-game separation
Trench play: Evaluating first-step quickness, lateral agility, and burst for offensive and defensive linemen during agility drills compared to pass-rush or run-blocking performance
Drill translation: Comparing sensor-based movement metrics with traditional drill times to identify where tracking data adds context to prospect evaluation
Position-specific mechanics: Analyzing body control, acceleration profiles, or pursuit angles during position drills and their relationship to NFL rookie performance Note that the above list is not exhaustive, and we encourage participants to be creative with their submissions.

Submission Requirements
Word and figure limits: Submissions should contain no more than 2,000 words and fewer than 10 tables or figures. Submissions over these limits may be subject to penalty.
Code reproducibility: Participants are encouraged to show statistical code if it helps readers better understand their analyses, but most code should be placed in an Appendix or linked notebook. Any writeup that does not use the player tracking data will not be scored.
Judges Panel
Judges will consist of analytics staffers working for the 32 NFL teams or player tracking vendors that work with NFL teams. Scores will be summarized using a weighted average based on the percentages above.

Dataset Description
Official Data Description, File Catalog, and Complete Column Dictionary
1. Overview of Datasets
The competition dataset contains high-frequency optical tracking, standardized physical testing results, and multi-season NFL game performance metrics for 510 rookie prospects drafted or signed between 2023 and 2025.

The dataset is partitioned into 9 CSV files (~2.2 GiB total) linking pre-draft physical traits directly to regular-season on-field outcomes.
